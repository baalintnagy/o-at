O-NEC, O-NR, O-AUD-TX, O-AUD-RX and O-AUD-A for Linux (x64)
============================================================

Contents
--------
VST3/O-NEC.vst3, O-NR.vst3, O-AUD-TX.vst3, O-AUD-RX.vst3, O-AUD-A.vst3   VST3 plugins
CLAP/O-NEC.clap, O-NR.clap, O-AUD-TX.clap, O-AUD-RX.clap, O-AUD-A.clap    CLAP plugins
O-AUD-RX                        Plays a bus on any output device
O-AUD-TX                        Sends input devices to buses
O-AUD-A-standalone              Analyzes an audio input device
O-NEC-standalone                O-NEC without a plugin host
O-NR-standalone                 O-NR without a plugin host

Requirements
------------
x86-64 Linux with glibc 2.35 or later (Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12 or newer), an X11 or
XWayland session, and the X11, OpenGL and ALSA libraries that desktop distributions
install: libx11-xcb1, libxcb1, libgl1 and libasound2.

Install the plugins
-------------------
1. Copy the folders in VST3 to ~/.vst3 and the files in CLAP to ~/.clap (for every user:
   /usr/lib/vst3 and /usr/lib/clap, with root rights):

       mkdir -p ~/.vst3 ~/.clap
       cp -r VST3/*.vst3 ~/.vst3/
       cp CLAP/*.clap ~/.clap/

2. Rescan plugins in your host. They are listed under the vendor "o-at".

The applications need no installation; run them from any folder, for example
./O-AUD-RX. The standalone plugins choose JACK when a JACK (or PipeWire JACK) server is
running and ALSA otherwise; pick one with --backend jack or --backend alsa (see --help).

Route audio between hosts and devices
-------------------------------------
1. Insert O-AUD-TX where audio should leave a host. It names its bus Bus 1, Bus 2 and
   so on; type another name, or click EDIT if the host keeps your keystrokes.
2. Play the bus with the O-AUD-RX plugin in any host, or with O-AUD-RX on any output
   device: pick the bus from the list or enter its name.
3. Raise Latency in O-AUD-RX if its Dropouts counter keeps rising.

Senders and receivers must run on the same computer as the same user. Buses live in
/dev/shm.

O-AUD-RX remembers its bus, device and settings in ~/.config/O-AUD-RX/settings.ini and
accepts --bus NAME and --device NAME.

O-AUD-TX sends routes, each from an input device to its own bus. + ADD and REMOVE under
ROUTES add and stop routes; pick the selected route's input under AUDIO SOURCE. Capturing
what an output device or a single application plays needs Windows; on Linux, send what
a device plays by choosing its PipeWire or PulseAudio monitor as the input (for example
with pavucontrol). It remembers its routes in ~/.config/O-AUD-TX/settings.ini and
accepts --bus NAME and --input NAME ("default" selects the system default device); repeat
them for more routes, for example --bus Voice --input default.

Remove noise
------------
Insert O-NR on any track. Play a passage where only the noise is heard, press LEARN,
then press STOP LEARNING: that spectrum becomes the noise profile, stored with the
session. Raise Reduction for more attenuation and Threshold to treat more as noise;
LISTEN REMOVED plays what is taken away. Without a profile, or with Adaptive, O-NR
tracks steady noise under the signal by itself. Higher Resolution separates hum and
other steady tones better but adds delay, which hosts compensate.

Analyze audio
-------------
Insert O-AUD-A anywhere in a chain; it passes the audio through unchanged. Its pages
show a spectrum analyzer, a sonogram, a stereo imager, an oscilloscope, an EBU R 128
loudness meter with target presets and a PLATFORMS table of what streaming platforms'
normalization does to the track, and 3D spectrum and stereo views; SPECTRAL overlays the
waveform on the sonogram. OVERVIEW shows the views you switch on: drag a view's title to
move it and the gaps between views to resize them. Click the button at the right of a
view's header, or right-click the diagram, for its SETTINGS and COLORS; THEME colors the
controls. Drag the window's lower right corner to resize it; FULL SCREEN needs Windows.

SOURCES adds buses from O-AUD-TX next to the input, each in its own color, with its
loudness in the panel. Every view picks its signals under SIGNALS at the top of its
settings. MATCH LEVEL compares spectrum curves at equal loudness, MASKING shades the
frequencies where two curves are about equally loud.

O-AUD-A-standalone analyzes an input device and plays nothing back (see --help for
backends, devices and rates).

Linux builds are tested with the automated tests and loaded by hand, not yet in many
hosts; please report problems at https://github.com/baalintnagy/o-at/issues.
